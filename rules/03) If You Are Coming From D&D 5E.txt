



OSK IS A D&D-5E-ADJACENT FRAMEWORK
	Open-Source Knights is built primarily from the concepts and structure of D&D 5E.
	However, it modifies or replaces many of D&D's character-building, combat, and resource-management rules.
	Many familiar terms remain, but they may function differently. 
	When OSK and D&D rules conflict, use the OSK rule.

CHARACTERS AND CLASSES
	Most characters have one Primary Class and one Secondary Class.
	Characters gain levels in both Classes simultaneously.
	Classes do not grant a fixed list of features at every level; rather, they define the types of Abilities a character may create.
	There are no subclasses, though players are free to create Abilities based on Source Abilities from relevant subclasses.
	OSK does not use Backgrounds as a separate mechanical character feature.
	A character's history, occupation, and culture remain part of their backstory, but do not automatically grant Skills or features.

ABILITY CARDS
	Class features, spells, maneuvers, and similar effects are represented by Ability cards.
	These Ability cards are player-created, and are generally represented by 3x5 index cards. 
	This book contains a set of "default" OSK cards, but players are highly encouraged to design their own.
	Player-created abilities are called "Designed Abilities", and are usually based on a corresponding "Source Ability" from one of the character's Classes.
	A Designed Ability should preserve its Source Ability's fantasy, function, or both - but it does not need to preserve its exact mechanics.

ATTRIBUTES AND SKILLS
	OSK uses the six familiar Attributes: STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, and CHA.
	Attributes are recorded as modifiers rather than scores.
	Skills (Animal Handling, Stealth, etc) are not permanently linked to specific Attributes.
	OSK does not use a Perception Skill. The Game Master should freely describe anything a character can plainly see, hear, smell, or otherwise notice.
	Hidden dangers and concealed information may involve Skills such as Investigation, Insight, or Survival.
	The Persuasion Skill represents a character's minimum competence in social influence.
	A Persuasion modifier is the "floor" that a corresponding Persuasion check should add at minimum.
	However, if a player employs strong reasoning or skilled roleplaying, the GM should grant a higher modifier, as appropriate.
	Note that even a very high Persuasion Check does not force another character to behave irrationally.

COMBAT IN BRIEF
	Combat in OSK assumes the use of a gridded map.
	Initiative is team-based, and play alternates between the Player Team and the Enemy Team.
	Abilities, Equipment, and Consumables are represented by cards.
	Only cards in the character's current Loadout are immediately available during combat. 
	Most Ability cards (and certain Equipment cards) enter the Cooldown Queue after being used.
	The Cooldown Queue replaces many resources such as spell slots, superiority dice, Ki points, and sorcery points.
	Critical Hits and Critical Misses in combat function a bit differently than D&D 5E.
	See the Combat chapter for the complete rules.

THE ACTION ECONOMY
	Each character takes three Actions on their turn. OSK does not divide a turn into an Action, Bonus Action, and Movement.
	A character may take the same action type up to twice on a turn.
	However, only one card can be placed into the Cooldown Queue per turn.
	Additionally, some cards can have a "Combo" property, which allows them to be played into Cooldown Slot 1 simultaneously with another card.
	OSK does not use a universal "Reaction".
	A character may instead Hold an Action and define an off-turn Trigger for it.
	OSK also does not use Attacks of Opportunity or a Disengage action.
	Specific Abilities may still create between-turn effects or restrict enemy movement.

DAMAGE AND WEAPONS
	OSK primarily uses d6s for damage.
	Ordinary weapons do not have separate universal damage dice. Rather, damage is primarily determined by the Ability being used.
	Ability damage is organized into five Damage Tiers, for balancing purposes.
	Physical damage combines bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing into one category of Damage Type.
	Unusual or powerful weapons may have their own Equipment cards.

ARMOR CLASS
	Each team shares a single Armor Class. Player AC is based on the team's current Level. 
	A roll below Armor Class does not usually mean a complete miss. Many Abilities have a weaker "Under-AC" result and a stronger "Over-AC" result.
	A character's standard armor is considered "built into" the default party AC.
	Special armor may have its own Equipment card, but this should generally raise the character's HP instead of adjusting AC.

OTHER IMPORTANT DIFFERENCES
	Advantage and Disadvantage can stack into Double Advantage and Double Disadvantage (roll 3 d20s and use the highest/lowest).
	Advantage and Disadvantage cancel each other one step at a time.
	A character may hold up to 5 Inspiration Points, which can be used to reroll failed Checks or Attack Rolls.
	Short Rests do not exist, and a Long Rest (also simply called a Rest) usually lasts 8-12 hours. 
	Healing is limited by Recovery Points rather than Hit Dice or Spell Slots.
	Any time a character recovers HP between Long Rests, no matter the amount, they spend a Recovery Point. 
	A dying character may still be stabilized even if they have no remaining Recovery Points.
	Resting restores all HP and Recovery Points. 

RULINGS OVER RULES
	OSK intentionally gives the Game Master broad authority. Intuition and common sense trump having an explicit rule for every situation.
	Custom Abilities, Wildcard Actions, and unusual situations rely on judgment rather than exhaustive procedures.
	The Game Master may revise a rule or custom card that proves unclear, disruptive, or unbalanced.
	Players and the Game Master should approach these decisions collaboratively.
