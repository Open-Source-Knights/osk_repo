EQUIPMENT:
	Equipment cards represent reusable gear, such as special weapons, armor, and other tools.
	Only unusual, specialized, or mechanically important items require their own Equipment card.
	Mundane clothing, weapons, tools, and supplies can usually be either grouped together on a single Equipment card, or simply written down as un-carded equipment.
	Some Equipment cards are activated similarly to Ability cards, by taking the Use Equipment action.
	Activating Equipment in combat may involve an Attack Roll, Saving Throw, Check, movement, damage, or another effect.

EQUIPMENT IN YOUR LOADOUT:
	An Equipment card usually must be in your Loadout for you to benefit from it during combat.
	Equipment in your Deck is carried by your character, but is not immediately available for use.
	Equipment can be swapped between your Deck and Loadout through the Reconfigure action.

EQUIPMENT COOLDOWN:
	Like most Ability cards, some Equipment cards require Cooldown after they are activated.
	When used, place the Equipment card into Cooldown Slot 1. The card moves through the Cooldown Queue normally.
	Equipment that does not say it requires Cooldown simply remains in the Loadout after use.

COMBO EQUIPMENT:
	Some Equipment cards have the Combo trait. A Combo Equipment card may enter Cooldown Slot 1 in addition to one other card used during the same turn.
	The Combo card may be placed above or below the other card, depending on which card was used first.
	A Combo card still requires a Use Equipment Action unless stated otherwise.
	For more rules on the Combo trait and the Cooldown Queue, see Chapter XX.

EQUIPMENT THAT MODIFIES ABILITIES:
	Equipment may modify compatible Ability cards while it remains in your Loadout.
	However, Equipment whose primary purpose is modifying Abilities should generally also have its own independent Ability-like effect as well. 
	This prevents the Equipment from becoming useless while its compatible Abilities are unavailable in the Cooldown Queue.
	For example, a magical fire sword might add fire damage to melee-based Abilities, while also providing its own attack when activated with the Use Equipment Action.
	These Ability-like effects may justify a Cooldown themselves, but that should NOT prevent the Equipment's passive effects from working as normal.
	Many types of Equipment (such as armor, tools, and other primarily passive items) may reasonably have no Ability-like use.

PASSIVE EQUIPMENT:
	Passive Equipment provides a continuous benefit while it remains in your Loadout. It does not require an Action to activate.
	Its benefit ends immediately if the card leaves your Loadout.
	Passive Equipment should clearly state what benefit it provides.

WEAPONS, ARMOR AND SHIELDS:
	Ordinary weapons do not have universal damage dice. The damage and effects of an attack are determined primarily by the Ability being used.
	A weapon may be part of an Ability's description without requiring its own Equipment card.
	Unusual, magical, advanced, or especially powerful weapons may have Equipment cards.
	Ordinary armor and shields are already represented by the team's shared Armor Class. They do not require separate Equipment cards.
	Special armor and shields generally do require Equipment cards. They may provide passive benefits or activated effects.
	Special armor and shields SHOULD NOT modify the team's Armor Class. Instead, they should provide a boost to the character's Hit Points. 
	Shields can also be used as part of a Wildcard action, such as bashing an enemy in the face, or sledding down stairs while shooting orcs with a bow.

PACKS AND KITS:
	A Pack or Kit represents a collection of related mundane gear. Packs and Kits can be represented by a single Equipment card.
	Examples include an adventuring pack, healer's kit, climbing kit, disguise kit, or artisan's tools.
	The player and Game Master should use common sense when deciding whether a reasonable item is included.
	Rare, expensive, magical, or unusually powerful items should be tracked separately.

FOUND EQUIPMENT:
	Characters may find or acquire Equipment during play.
	Unless the Equipment is mundane or inconsequential enough to be un-carded, a newly acquired item is added to the character's Deck.
	During combat, a character can place a newly-acquired item directly into their Loadout. (If it causes the player's Loadout to go beyond 10, the player must move one card into their Deck.)
	An unfamiliar item may require a familiarization, analysis, activation, or special instruction before it can be used effectively.

UN-CARDED EQUIPMENT:
	Trivial, mundane, or inconsequential minor items may not require a full Equipment card. The player can simply make a note that they've acquired the item.
	Examples include small trinkets, low-value gems, everyday utensils, edible fruits, etc.

EQUIPMENT LIMITATIONS:
	OSK generally avoids tracking mundane ammunition, such as arrows.
	However, certain kinds of special Equipment may require ammunition, fuel, charges, two hands, a free hand, a specific environment, or something else in order to be usable.
	Any important limitation should be written on the Equipment card.
	Certain limitations, such as special ammunition, may be represented by Consumable cards in addition to the corresponding Equipment card.

DESIGNING EQUIPMENT CARDS:
	Like Ability cards, Equipment cards should be concise and fit on a 3x5 card.
	The card should state whether it is Activated, Passive, Combo, Consumable, or Requires Cooldown. The card should also define any compatible Abilities, range, targets, rolls, effects, and limitations.
	Equipment should be balanced using principles similar to Ability design (see Chapter XX). In other words, equipment that grants a broad or permanent benefit should generally be weaker than a comparable activated effect.
	The GM may revise Equipment that proves unclear, disruptive, or unbalanced.

CONSUMABLES:
	Consumables are one-time-use items, represented by Consumable cards.
	Examples include potions, scrolls, special ammunition, alchemical mixtures, poisons, and enchanted food.
	After a Consumable is used, remove its card from the character's Deck.

USING CONSUMABLES:
	Activating a Consumable normally requires a Use Consumable action. Consumables usually do not enter the Cooldown Queue.
	Unless the card states otherwise, a consumable must be in your Loadout in order to use it. After you've used it, you can immediately swap a new card from your Deck into your Loadout.
	A Consumable may be used on yourself, an adjacent creature, or another valid target described by the card.
	Throwable, offensive Consumables may require an Attack Roll, Check, or defined area of effect.

CONSUMABLE EFFECTS:
	Consumables may restore HP, grant Temporary HP, remove Conditions, modify Abilities, create environmental effects, or produce other benefits.
	A Consumable should state any relevant secondary information, such as range, target, duration, required rolls, etc.
	Healing Consumables still require the target to spend a Recovery Point, unless the card specifically says otherwise.
	Exceptionally powerful Consumables (such as ones that restore Recovery Points or resurrect dead allies) should generally be extraordinarily rare.
