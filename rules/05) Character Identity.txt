

OSK'S CHARACTER-DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
	Much of OSK's mechanically streamlined design is intended specifically to allow creativity to flourish in other ways.
	Less mental exertion toward understanding rules means more capacity for embodying your character. 
	Your character is more than their Species, Classes, Attributes, Skills, and Abilities.
	That being said, you do not need to write a long biography before play begins. A few clear ideas are usually enough to make them feel distinct and believable.

BACKSTORY AND GOALS:
	Consider where your character came from and which events shaped them.
	Think about why they became an adventurer and why they chose their Classes.
	Decide whether they have important allies, enemies, debts, promises, or unfinished business.
	Ask: "What does my character want right now?"
	Goals should encourage action rather than give the character a reason to avoid the adventure.

BELIEFS AND PERSONALITY:
	Consider what your character believes is right, wrong, sacred, dangerous, or necessary.
	Ask: "How would my character feel about authority, religion, magic, violence, wealth, or social status?"
	Think about how they speak, react, make decisions, and behave under pressure.
	Ask: "What makes my character angry, afraid, embarrassed, or excited?"
	Your character's beliefs may be conflicted and can change over time.

RELATIONSHIPS, STRENGTHS, AND FLAWS:
	Consider how your character treats strangers, allies, rivals, enemies, and authority figures.
	Ask: "Who does my character trust?"
	Ask: "What kind of person does my character dislike?"
	Give your character strengths beyond their mechanical abilities.
	Also give them flaws, fears, habits, or blind spots.
	Flaws should create interesting choices without constantly preventing the group from making progress.

DETAILS AND EMBODIMENT:
	Small details can make your character easier to portray.
	Consider their clothing, posture, gestures, hobbies, routines, tastes, and possessions.
	You can speak in character, or describe their words indirectly - or even use a mixture of both.
	You do not need to perform an accent or act dramatically.
	Ask: "What would my character notice, feel, say, or do in this situation?"

CHARACTER GROWTH:
	Your character does not need to remain the same throughout the campaign.
	Their goals, beliefs, relationships, and behavior may change during play.
	Failure, success, loss, friendship, and conflict can all shape them.
	Leave room for new discoveries and allow your character to surprise you.

ALIGNMENT:
	Feel free to think of your character's position on the standard 9-square alignment chart, with a "lawful vs chaotic" axis and a "good vs bad" axis.
	However, a character's alignment has almost no mechanical implications. It serves more of a guideline for roleplaying.
